# Wedge — the narrow, provable claim

The output is a single sentence that makes an experienced operator think "specific — tell me more." Not a title, not a stack, not a list of adjectives.

## Why self-assessment fails

Two consistent errors. Users overweight what they enjoy, and they underweight what feels easy to them. The second matters more: something they can do in a day that takes peers weeks reads to them as trivial because they calibrate against themselves, not against the market.

Work from evidence and outside observation, not introspection.

## Extract episodes, not adjectives

"I am strategic" carries no information. "I ran the pricing rework that moved ARPU from 18 to 31 in six weeks" does.

Ask in one round, each question with the best inference from prior context attached:

- Walk through the most recent thing you built or shipped where a measurable outcome moved. Which number, by how much?
- What have people asked you to look at, review, or unblock — including things you were not paid or credited for?
- In your last three roles, what fell to you regardless of your job description?
- When you look at peers in this discipline, what do you do differently that they seem not to?
- What have you shipped in a weekend that others estimated in weeks?
- What do most people in this field misunderstand about it?

Push once on vague answers with a specific example request. Do not push twice. Missing numbers are an evidence gap — handle via `starting-from-zero.md`, do not invent proxies.

## Extract the mechanism, not the role

Look across episodes for the repeated mechanism, not the repeated job title. Episodes like "grew a Discord to twelve thousand," "wrote a launch post that produced four thousand signups," and "fixed a friend's activation flow and doubled first-week retention" are not "marketing." They are *getting a cold audience to take a first action*. That level of specificity is what makes the claim saleable.

Test each candidate claim against three filters:

1. Narrow enough to be plausible? "Growth" is not. "Taking B2C mobile apps from zero to their first ten thousand organic users via short-form video" is.
2. Does someone pay when it works? If the line from skill to revenue takes more than one hop, it is a hobby in this context.
3. Provable with a specific past result? Not "years of experience." A number, a shipped thing, a person by name.

Leave with one to three candidate claims. More than three means the extraction was not sharp enough.

## Validate outside the user's head

Self-validated claims are worth little. Have the user send messages to five to eight specific ex-colleagues, in their voice, with open questions. Never ask "am I good at X" — polite people confirm.

Better prompts:

- Think of a specific project we worked on. What did I do differently from what other people might have done in that seat?
- If a friend were starting a project in this space, what specific thing would you tell them to hire me for?
- Where did I make your job harder, and where did I make it easier?
- What did I do that surprised you?

Read the responses with the user. Look for words that repeat across senders. Those words are the claim, and often not the words the user would have picked themselves.

## Testing competing claims

If two or three candidates survive validation, do not debate — test cheaply:

- Draft the same trial three ways, one per claim, and see which gets replies from the same class of company.
- Post one short teardown per claim and watch which pulls DMs.
- Mention each in the first three conversations and notice which one makes people lean in.

Interest from the right people is signal. Applause from the wrong people is not.

## "I am good at a lot of things"

Treat this as an honest statement of two assets: time and willingness. Real, but priced far below an edge. Two moves:

1. Borrow a constraint. Pick the domain with the most raw material and commit to it for six weeks minimum. Breadth becomes an edge only when depth gives it somewhere to attach.
2. Convert generality into a scoped promise. Not "I can help with anything" — "I will run your outbound experiments for six weeks and hand you a working channel or a clear no." Generalists win on ownership of an unowned problem. Name the problem.

## Output

Fill the positioning brief from SKILL.md. Do not skip evidence with names attached — that is what makes downstream outreach hold up — and do not skip the claims to drop. Every unprovable claim drowns out the provable ones.
