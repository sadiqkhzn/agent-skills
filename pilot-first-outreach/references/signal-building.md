# Signal — inbound artifacts

Most guides on this topic skip this workstream, which is a mistake. Public output over eight to twelve weeks does two things outbound cannot:

- It compounds. One teardown that lands well produces ten to twenty warm inbound conversations. No amount of outbound produces that ratio.
- It fixes the credibility problem before the first message. By the time the user DMs a founder, that founder can search the user's name and see months of thinking about exactly this problem. The DM is no longer cold.

Outbound alone works. Outbound with signal underneath it works three to five times better per hour spent.

## What to publish

Publish work adjacent to the map. If the map contains DTC coffee brands with weak retention, the user's output is about DTC retention mechanics — not general marketing, not the user's career journey.

Three formats, in decreasing depth:

- **Long-form teardown, weekly.** Pick a specific company from the map, publicly analyze their funnel, name what works and what is being left on the table. Include numbers where knowable. This is the load-bearing artifact — everything else derives from it.
- **Short-form derivative, three or four per week.** Pull one insight from the teardown, present it standalone. Each links back to the long form.
- **Substantive replies, daily.** Reply to founders' public posts with a counter-observation, a competitor example, or a number they may not have seen. Not "great post." Reply until it becomes obvious to the founder that this person has been paying attention for weeks.

## What not to publish

- Anything about the user personally that is not directly relevant to the claim
- Frameworks without an example
- Predictions about the market with no data
- Reposts without meaningful commentary
- Career announcements
- Motivational content of any kind

The bar: if a founder in the map read only one of the user's posts and nothing else, would they conclude that this person has spent real time inside their problem? If not, do not publish it.

## How this feeds the other workstreams

- Teardowns become outreach. The teardown of company X is the observation in the DM to company X's founder. The message is short because most of it is already public and linked.
- Reactions become the map. Founders who engage with signal work are hot leads — they have self-identified as caring about this problem. Add them.
- Signal work reveals what to sharpen. If posts on retention get replies and posts on acquisition do not, that is data on where the wedge actually lives. Take it back to the wedge workstream.

## Volume vs. quality

The ratio matters more than the frequency. Ten weekly posts where eight are lazy trains the audience to skip everything. Two weekly posts where both are sharp does not.

If the user cannot sustain a weekly teardown at real depth, cut to one every two weeks. Do not manufacture volume for the algorithm.

## When to skip this workstream

- User already has a large existing audience in the target market
- User is running a fixed four-to-six-week hunt with a hard deadline (signal is a slow game)
- User's target is a single specific company — all-in on outbound and deep preparation for that one

For everyone else, running signal in parallel is not optional if the goal is repeatable inbound over time.

## Output

A weekly plan: one teardown topic, three derivative post outlines, five founders whose posts to engage with substantively. Outlines only, not full drafts — the user writes the actual posts.
