# Market mapping — from claim to specific companies

A claim is worth nothing in the abstract. It is worth something at a specific company where it moves a number that matters. The same claim is worth ten times more to a business with high LTV and a leaky top of funnel than to one with a strong funnel and no monetization.

## The chain

Walk it in order.

1. **Claim** — from the wedge workstream.
2. **Result inside a business** — the outcome, not the activity. "Content strategy" is an activity. "A repeatable acquisition channel producing trials under X CAC" is a result.
3. **Money math** — how the result becomes revenue or saved cost, written as arithmetic: `traffic to signup, then conversion, then ARPU, then LTV`. Rough is fine.
4. **Where the number is the current binding constraint** — business models where the result matters most.
5. **Companies that need it and are not running it** — the actual list.

Most users rush step five. The point is not companies that could use the claim. It is companies where the gap is visible, the market has proven the play, and nobody inside owns it.

## Finding the visible gap

Research each candidate until a specific missing thing can be named. Reliable sources:

- Competitors — check five to ten direct competitors. If most run the play and this one does not, that is the gap.
- Dead channels — a blog last updated two years ago, three total posts on a TikTok account, a referral program with no incentive.
- The funnel, walked as a user. Sign up. Where does it slow, break, or go silent? Does the welcome email arrive?
- Hiring signals. A role open four months means they know the gap and cannot fill it. A role just filled means the gap is closed — drop the company.
- What the founder says publicly. Podcasts, X threads, launch retrospectives.
- Recent funding plus growth mandate plus small team equals unowned problems.

Verify recency. Claiming a company is missing something they shipped last month kills the conversation instantly.

## Qualification

Two-stage filter, not a scorecard. Scorecards let borderline candidates through when several weak signals add up to a passing total.

**Stage one: must-haves.** Any missing, drop the company.

- The gap is specific and verifiable from public sources
- There is a credible path — warm intro, mutual context, or referenceable public engagement — to one person who can decide alone
- The company has enough traction that a lever will produce measurable results within four to six weeks

**Stage two: rank survivors.** Score one to five on each, sum, approach the top three first.

- Evidence match — how directly the user's past work maps to this specific gap
- Momentum — how fast the decider moves from conversation to trial (founder-led SMB beats mid-market beats enterprise)
- Value density — potential impact divided by user effort required

Two shape filters:

- Prefer companies with existing traction over pre-seed. Distribution and product already exist, so a lever applied there produces measurable results fast — which is what the user needs for the next case study.
- Prefer founder-led SMB and mid-market over Fortune 500. The mechanism requires a single decider; large enterprises do not have them.

## Research checklist, per company

Fifteen focused minutes is enough for a first pass:

- What they sell, to whom, how they charge
- Stage, team size, last raise, growth signal
- The specific missing thing, with the evidence trail
- Two or three named competitors running the thing, with public signal on how it is working
- Rough impact estimate — state the assumption, do not fake precision
- Decider name, public positioning, warm path if any
- Shared context: same market, mutual connection, shared past employer

## Output

Ten well-researched rows beat fifty guessed. Rank by the stage-two score. Flag the top three and explain why — usually where warm access intersects with strongest evidence fit.

Keep one honest column: **the weakest point in this case.** A user who knows the counterargument handles pushback calmly instead of freezing.
