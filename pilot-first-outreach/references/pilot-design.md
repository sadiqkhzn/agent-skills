# Pilot design — the scoped, reversible trial

The pilot solves one problem: the founder needs to see whether this person is actually good, and no interview or reference call answers that. A trial does.

The user's job is to design the trial so the founder has no rational reason to refuse: bounded time, defined success, downside carried by the user, upside earned only if the number moves.

## Design principles

1. **Small enough to fit in a corner of the founder's month.** If it demands more than a few hours of their team's attention per week, it dies.
2. **Reversible.** Both sides walk at the end of the window without invoices or damage.
3. **Measurable in one number.** Subjective success drains all the goodwill from discovery into a debate over what "worked" means.
4. **Short enough to finish before enthusiasm decays.** Four to six weeks. Beyond that, reprioritization kills it.
5. **Priced so the user has skin in the game.** Zero-cost trials look suspicious; large-cost trials trigger procurement. Middle ground: a modest fixed fee at a discount rate, upside conditional on the measured result.

## Scoping template

Fill in with the user:

- **Hypothesis** — one sentence. "If we do X, Y will move by Z in W weeks."
- **Scope** — three to seven bullets, specific enough that the founder knows what they get
- **Out of scope** — bullets. Protects against scope creep, which kills more trials than anything else
- **Measurement** — the single number, how it is tracked, who has access, when it is read
- **Timeline** — start, checkpoints if any, end, what happens on the end date
- **Company cost** — fixed fee plus any tooling, access, or team time required
- **Success terms** — what extension looks like (retainer, equity, role), triggered at what threshold on the measured number
- **Failure terms** — what happens if the number does not move: both parties walk, no invoice, non-disparagement optional

Failure terms matter more than success terms. They are what makes the founder say yes.

## Pricing models

Three that work, in order of user-friendliness:

- **Fixed fee plus success bonus.** Small upfront below market rate for the effort, plus a bonus tied to the measured result. Best for users who need income now and have strong evidence.
- **Equity trial into a potential role.** No cash. Small equity or options grant vests at the end if the metric is hit. Best for users who can defer income and want in on the company long-term.
- **Pure success fee.** No upfront, only paid if the metric moves. Best for users with weak evidence needing to overcome the credibility gap. Only works with terms tight enough that the founder cannot drag the trial out for free.

Do not offer a full retainer or full-time salary on trial one. That is asking to be evaluated, which is exactly what this method avoids.

## What the founder will push back on

- **Attribution.** "How do we know your work moved the number?" Answer this before they ask. Pre-agree on measurement window, baseline, and isolation method (holdout, A/B, geo split, whatever fits the case).
- **IP.** "Do we own what you build?" Default: they own everything produced during the trial. Anything the user built before is theirs. State it explicitly.
- **Exclusivity.** "Can you work with competitors?" Default: not during the trial window and for ninety days after if it converts. Not longer — the user has a career to run.
- **Confidentiality.** Sign a light NDA. Refuse a heavy one — heavy NDAs signal a founder who does not move fast.

## When they push for a full role instead

Redirect:

> "Happy to talk about that once we know the thing works. Let us run the trial, and if the number moves we will have a much better conversation about what the role actually looks like."

Correct for both sides. The user avoids being hired on hope; the founder avoids hiring on hope.

## Reopening a stalled conversation

If the founder went quiet after a good call, this is the soft reopener:

> "I put down what a small test would look like based on our conversation — four weeks, one measured outcome, no cost if it does not move the number. Sending in case it is useful. No pressure if the timing is not right."

Attach the one-page proposal. Do not follow up on the reopener. Either it lands or it does not.

## Output

A one-page pilot proposal per interested company, drafted only when a conversation warrants it. Not written speculatively — writing a pilot for someone who has not shown interest wastes the user's time and cheapens the artifact.
