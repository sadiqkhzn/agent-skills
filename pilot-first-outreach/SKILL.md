# Pilot-first outreach

The user is not solving a hiring problem. They are solving a trust problem: the person on the receiving end has no cheap way to verify they are any good. Applications, CVs, and "let us explore synergies" pitches all ask the same question — trust me — and get the same answer.

This skill inverts the ask. The user shows up with a named revenue gap at a named company, evidence they can close it, and a small reversible trial that lets the company find out without commitment.

## Four workstreams, not five steps

Most guides on this topic teach a linear pipeline. That misleads users who need to run eight conversations at once. Real practice is four parallel workstreams, each on its own cadence:

| Workstream | Cadence | Question it answers |
|---|---|---|
| Wedge | Quarterly review, rewritten if outreach stops converting | What narrow claim can the user prove? |
| Map | Weekly, three to five new candidates | Which specific companies leak revenue for the lack of this? |
| Signal | Daily public artifacts, weekly outbound | How do the right people discover the user exists? |
| Trial | Per opportunity, drafted only after interest | What is the smallest thing that proves the thesis? |

The user does not finish one before starting the next. Signal work runs while the wedge is still being sharpened. Map research feeds signal topics. A trial drafted for company A teaches what to filter for at company B.

## Meet the user where they are

Do not restart the loop on someone with a call already booked.

- Cannot name their strength — `references/wedge.md`
- Knows the strength, not who pays for it — `references/market-mapping.md`
- List exists, no replies — `references/signal-building.md`, then reassess outreach
- Call is booked — `references/discovery-calls.md`
- Founder is interested — `references/pilot-design.md`
- No provable track record yet — `references/starting-from-zero.md`, before any outreach
- Applied to eighty jobs with no reply — wedge first; the funnel is not broken, the input is

## Interview in batches

The user's specifics are required. Do not extract them by drip-questioning.

Ask every question that does not depend on an answer still pending as a numbered list, each with the best inference from prior context attached. The user replies "1 correct, 2 wrong, 3 skip" in seconds. A wrong inference gets corrected faster than an open question gets answered.

Never ask for anything researchable. What a target company sells, who its competitors are, whether the role is open, what the founder said publicly — go find it. Ask the user only for what only the user knows: history, constraints, preferences, access.

Stop interviewing the moment the current workstream's artifact can be produced. Further questions are procrastination.

## Evidence discipline

The whole method depends on the user being able to say "I can do X because I already did Y, here is the number." No Y, no outreach — send them to `references/starting-from-zero.md`. Founder attention is a one-shot resource; unbacked claims burn it.

Never fabricate a Y for them. Invented metrics are checked on the first call by any competent operator, and the market is small enough that being caught follows the user for years.

## Default artifacts

Produce things the user can act on the same day.

**Wedge — positioning brief.** The claim in one sentence, three pieces of evidence with names attached, three validation questions to send to ex-colleagues, two claims to stop making.

**Map — scored target list, ten rows.** Company, what they sell, the named missing thing, evidence the missing thing works in this market, rough revenue impact, decider access route, weakest point in the case. Ranked, top three flagged.

**Signal — weekly artifact plan.** One long-form public teardown, three derivative shorter posts, five founders whose public work the user should be engaging with substantively.

**Trial — one-page pilot.** Hypothesis, scope, out-of-scope, single measured outcome, timeline, cost to the company, terms on success, terms on failure.

## The one-paragraph brief

Before any outreach to a specific company, the user must be able to say the following without hesitating. If it cannot be drafted from research, the research is incomplete — go back to the map.

> Company X sells [product] to [segment]. Their [named number] is [current state]. Competitors A, B, and C moved that number by running [play]. If it works here, the effect is roughly [magnitude]. The user has run this play at [prior context] and produced [specific number].

## Two failure modes

**Pitching on call one.** The moment the user says "I could do this for you," everything before it retroactively becomes a job pitch. The founder should leave the first call thinking about the opportunity, not the candidate. Let them arrive at "we have nobody for this" on their own.

**Volume without depth.** Twenty specific approaches outperform four hundred templated ones, and being known as a mass-DM sender costs more than it earns in a small market. Scale the research, not the sending.

## Tone

Match the user's own voice. Founders skip anything that reads like a consulting deck. Short sentences, specific nouns, no adjectives doing the work of evidence. Leave the user with something they can send in ten minutes, not a framework to think about over the weekend.
